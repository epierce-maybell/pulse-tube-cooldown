Stage cross-coupling: focused checks, 2026-11-06

Status: no validated physical correction yet. The current equations can give
positive dQ1/dT2 on a stable branch, but the tested parameter changes do not
recover the measured combination of stage responses. This is not a proof
that no parameter fit can succeed. No production equations or parameter
files were changed.

Convention and operating point

Q is heat into a stage, so balancing heater power is -Q. Every derivative
below holds the other stage and warm boundary fixed, re-equilibrates the
matrix, and includes the manifold pressure response. The operating point
is T0 = 295 K, T1 = 58 K, T2 = 3.4 K, using the template's head and drive.
Cooler-only heat flows exclude the separate shield radiation link.
At fixed T1 that link contributes zero to dQ1/dT2.

The archived baseline branch differs from the other session's reported
branch: here dQ1/dT2 is -0.425 W/K, rather than approximately -0.8 to -0.93.
Both have the wrong sign. The matrix equilibrium residual is 3.34e-11 W.
Its largest real matrix eigenvalue is -0.003074 /s, and with stages free
it is -0.002271 /s. The wrong coupling therefore also exists on a locally
stable branch, independently of the unstable no-load root in report.txt.

F1 at the cold boundary contains pressure flow, regeneration, and wall
conduction. It must not be identified entirely with pressure-driven flow.
Here its absolute value is 74.208 W: pressure 7.800 W, regeneration 65.192 W,
and wall conduction 1.216 W. This makes the upper-regenerator loss law,
alongside the shared drive, a relevant target for further model work.

Holding pressures fixed still gives dQ1/dT2 = -0.26559 W/K. Freezing only
the stage-1 regenerator mass-flow feedback, at the same equilibrium, gives
-0.23322 W/K with self-consistent pressures, or +0.29122 W/K with pressures
also fixed. Neither simplification is a physical correction. These checks
show that correcting the shared drive alone is insufficient at this point.

One bounded local fit and why it was rejected

Two targets were interpolated within S0009's May load map using linear
interpolation over triangles in the (T1, T2) plane:
  (50 K, 3.2 K): heaters (14.49021 W, 0.479536 W)
  (50 K, 3.8 K): heaters (13.39929 W, 0.872421 W)
They require an average dQ1/dT2 = +1.81821 W/K over that interval.

One simultaneous least-squares fit of four positive parameters and both
matrix profiles was capped at 80 function evaluations (about 128 s).
The remaining matrix imbalances reached 0.0413 W; the largest total
residual was 0.0723 W. Those numbers are not acceptable equilibria.
After re-solving the matrix at the first point with the fitted parameters,
Q1 changes from approximately -14.486 W to -12.320 W, and dQ1/dT2 is
-4.078 W/K. The second point still fails the equilibrium tolerance.
The apparent improvement exploited unbalanced matrix nodes. The full
rejected result is in rejected_local_fit.json. It is not evidence of an
acceptable fit or proof that fitting cannot work.

Recommended next step for the canonical fit

1. Solve the internal balances to the existing 1e-6 W tolerance at each
   fixed-plate load-map point before accepting or differentiating it.
   Treat matrix balances as constraints, rather than equally weighted
   observations that can trade against heater residuals. An implicit
   derivative of the converged internal solution avoids repeated inner
   solves for each temperature perturbation.
2. Check both dQ1/dT2 and dQ2/dT1, the two diagonal responses, and local
   thermal stability against the observed load-map behavior. Correcting
   only the sign of one derivative is insufficient. Fit heater powers;
   use measured slopes as validation of the fitted surface.
3. If a fit satisfying those conditions still fails, revisit the upper
   regenerator's transport/flow approximation and shared drive together.
   A revised oscillatory-flow model would require an ADR decision. No
   particular replacement has yet been demonstrated to fix both couplings;
   adding a fitted cross-temperature term or reversing F2's sign would
   bypass the present physical requirements.

Executable handoff

coupling.py supplies analyze_coupling(graph, temperature, stages=...,
fixed_boundary_nodes=...). It rejects an unbalanced internal matrix and
computes the reduced stage Jacobian by implicit differentiation. It also
reports fixed-stage and constant-heater stability. test_coupling.py checks
the derivative against an independently soluble three-conductance network
and verifies rejection of a non-equilibrium state.

From the repository root:
  .venv/Scripts/python -m simulations.validation.test_stand_steady_state.check_cases
  .venv/Scripts/python -m pytest simulations/validation/test_stand_steady_state/test_coupling.py tests/engine/test_steady_state.py -q -p no:cacheprovider

The first command rechecks five archived equilibrium states and parameter
choices, without fitting or a search. The second passes six tests. Ruff
lint and formatting checks pass for this experiment's Python files.

Additional repair investigation

The earlier SN09 baseline reproduced the forward coupling's sign, but its
stage-2 response was too steep. The later finite-capacity/exchanger fit
also retained a coupling tradeoff. These existing records are in
sn09_load_map/coupling_findings.txt, comparison.txt, and adr_investigation/;
they do not supply a complete replacement to copy into the present model.

A targeted check of the existing breakthrough-loss branch used a scalar
root for R1 such that L1/E1 = 0.9 at the placeholder operating profile,
then re-solved all matrix temperatures. R1 = 3.1159382e8 Pa s/m^3, with
the remaining parameters unchanged. In 11 evaluations the matrix residual
fell to 6.16e-11 W. At T1 = 58 K, T2 = 3.4 K the result has Q1 = +20.07 W,
Q2 = -0.8613 W, E1 = 1736.19 W, L1 = 1562.02 W, dQ1/dT2 = -1.7823 W/K.
This particular loss-dominated branch does not provide the repair. It is
one directed check of an existing mechanism, not a parameter survey.

A useful conservation constraint on the repair follows by summing the two
stage balances at matrix equilibrium:
  Q1 + Q2 = F1_cold + L1 + L2 + tube_wall1 + tube_wall2 - E1 - E2.
Here F1_cold is total energy arriving from the first regenerator section,
L1 and L2 are tube losses, and the tube_wall terms are heat conducted into
the stages. At fixed T1, the measured positive dQ1/dT2 and negative
dQ2/dT2 imply a positive derivative of their sum near the representative
one-compressor point. The placeholder gives -0.9169 W/K at 58 K / 3.4 K.
Redistributing a term solely between the two stage balances cannot repair
this summed response. Work must address the upstream energy flow, tube
losses, or delivered acoustic powers consistently with that balance.
