One amplitude-dependent harmonic/mean-equilibrium check, 2026-11-06

Decision: this version has not earned a production replacement. Adding the
minimum amplitude-dependent transport closures produces a formal balanced
matrix profile, but its temperature oscillations violate the first-order
approximation decisively. No coupling slope from it would be meaningful.
No further mechanisms or parameter searches were added to this study.

What was tried

One operating point: T0/T1/T2=295/58/3.4 K, fixed mean pressure1.565067 MPa,
fixed sinusoidal inlet peak pressure1 MPa, frequency1 Hz, and the existing
valve resistances(1e10,3e10) Pa s/m^3. The starting matrix temperatures are
the archived current-model equilibrium. This is a fixed-drive diagnostic;
charge and compressor supply were not solved. The inlet amplitude was
specified directly, without a square-wave factor. It is already64% of the
mean pressure, so pressure linearization is also a significant limitation.

The central previous geometry assumptions were retained:0.3 mm spheres,
40 um wire, sphere/screen porosities.38/.70, and screen tortuosity2.
They are not measurements of this PT1.5. No parameters were fitted.

Local effective drag is Darcy plus the fundamental of Ergun's quadratic
drag for sinusoidal mass flow:
 r_eff=r_D+[8/(3*pi)]*b*|m_hat|
 b=1.75*(1-phi)/(phi^3*dp*rho*Ab^2).
Heat transfer uses Nu=2+1.1*Re^.6*Pr^(1/3), with the local peak mass
amplitude used in Re. This is a quasi-steady, peak-value approximation
using a steady packed-bed correlation, not a validated oscillatory-flow
correlation. The screen uses its estimated hydraulic diameter in the same
simple closure; that extension is another explicit approximation.

The harmonic coefficients were updated by one damped local fixed-point
iteration(60% new amplitude), bounded at20 iterations. Mean equilibrium
used the original13 matrix temperatures as unknowns, bounded to2.5-295 K,
with least_squares limited to30 function evaluations plus its numerical
Jacobian evaluations and a55-second wall-time deadline. There was one
initial guess and one mean-equilibrium attempt, without continuation or
alternative branches.

Matrix nodes share ONE energy flux at each physical face. Adjacent cell
endpoint estimates are averaged at that face, then used by both nodes.
Existing wall conduction is retained. The stage balances retain ideal
pulse-tube acoustic removal, tube-wall conduction, and the existing smooth
mass-buffer breakthrough expression using solved reservoir mass amplitude.
No new loss mechanism was introduced. The spatial discretization is only
a feasibility mesh and was not convergence-studied after the validity gate
failed.

Results

The nonlinear harmonic solve converged at both profiles. At the final one:
  harmonic iterations                         12 of20
  relative mass-amplitude iteration change     1.15e-5
  maximum matrix heat imbalance               7.1e-11 W
  stage1 heat INTO stage                      +13.96 W
  stage2 heat INTO stage                      +16.74 W
  tube acoustic powers                        38.75,9.46 W
  stage pressure amplitudes / inlet           .881,.600
  tube-wall heat                              1.020,.256 W
  breakthrough heat                           .000141,.0000315 W
  inlet mass-flow peak                        .03137 kg/s

The tiny reported imbalance is the numerical residual of this discretized
closure with its bounded inner iteration; it is not physical accuracy.
Its matrix interior temperatures span4.575-198.835 K. The stage2 matrix
starts with59.39 K next to the58 K stage and then falls to4.575 K next
to the3.4 K stage. Each regenerator's common energy flux is constant to
the achieved residual:77.63 W in stage1 and25.94 W in stage2. The
telescoping energy balance gives
 Q1+Q2+sum(matrix heat)=F1_warm-E1-E2+walls+breakthrough,
which is also consistent with the reported30.70 W net stage heating.
No positive heater loads can balance the positive stage heat flows at
this operating point under the diagnostic's fixed drive.

The decisive failure is amplitude validity:
  max |theta_g|/T in regenerator1              .173
  max |theta_g|/T in regenerator2              21.63
  max |theta_m|/T in regenerator2              21.63
  largest absolute gas temperature peak       213.9 K

The maximum ratio is in the GOS region at x=.22922 m from stage2
regenerator's warm end(length.2445 m). Its cell coefficient mean
temperature is9.18 K; the mean profile at that face is8.54 K; the
gas temperature peak is198.7 K. Thus the failure occurs near9 K,
not solely in the3.4 K property extrapolation. Gas and matrix move
together there, so simply increasing H cannot restore small amplitudes.
A first-order sinusoid would imply grossly unphysical temperatures during
part of a cycle. A frozen-coefficient scaling would require over216-fold
drive reduction to make this maximum ratio below.1. That is only an
illustration: r and H themselves depend on amplitude, so it is not an
operating prediction or an additional calculated case.

Stability: not evaluated because the first-order amplitude gate fails.
Relaxed forward/reverse coupling: not evaluated for the same reason.
There are no partial slopes presented as equilibrium slopes. Formal matrix
balance alone does not make these thermodynamic oscillations admissible.

Cost and stopping reason

The single mean solve completed in26.94 s, before its55 s deadline, with
126 actual residual evaluations including finite-difference Jacobians.
One replay of that same result to extract amplitude-location metadata took
.35 s. No extra operating point was solved. Work stopped because the
balanced solution fails the small-amplitude assumption by over two orders
of magnitude relative to a.1 ratio criterion.

Complexity assessment

This network could replace the current separate acoustic propagation,
scalar mass-swing regeneration loss, and empirical packing coefficient a
with a common complex pressure/flow solution and physically estimated drag
and heat transfer. That is a coherent reason to consider it. However,
even the minimal version requires an amplitude iteration nested inside a
mean matrix solve, uncertain microgeometry, and correlations whose
oscillatory applicability has not been established. Here it has delivered
neither a valid operating cycle nor evidence for the measured asymmetric
stage coupling. Those benefits do not justify production integration.

No higher-H or reduced-inertia variant was attempted: the primary model
failed its validity gate. No more mechanisms are proposed as part of this
study. The present first-order model should be set aside as an unvalidated
replacement, rather than becoming the starting point for a broad fit.

Reproduction and sources

Run from the repository root:
 .venv/Scripts/python -m simulations.validation.test_stand_steady_state.phase_feasibility.balanced
The isolated code is balanced.py and saved output is balanced_results.json.
The prior report.txt records the geometry assumptions, real-gas/material
sources, units, and correlation references. Those assumptions remain in
force. In particular, HEPAK's expansion_coefficient output is beta*T,
not beta; the equations use it accordingly. Its conductivity below4 K
is held at the4 K value in this diagnostic because the3.4 K output is
zero; low-temperature viscosity was not independently validated.

  combined temperature amplitude             198.685 K

  density                                    104.05 kg/m^3
  pore area                                  .0003056 m^2
  mean temperature gradient                 -842.43 K/m
  gas capacity per length                    218.40 J/(m K)
  matrix capacity per length                  45.96 J/(m K)
  gas-matrix conductance per length           170383 W/(m K)

The inferred gas-displacement amplitude |m|/(omega*rho*Ag) is.284 m,
whereas the local mean-temperature length T/|dT/dx| is.0109 m.
In the strong-exchange limit the gradient term is approximately
  |theta_gradient| = [Cg/(Cg+Cm)] * |displacement*dT/dx|.
These values reproduce the roughly198 K result. The linearized local
gradient is being used far beyond its small-displacement range. This is
not a credible prediction that the real matrix oscillates through200 K.
HEPAK cp agrees with the production mass-specific-heat accessor; the
beta*T convention was checked. Cell transfer equations reproduce their
stored end states with relative error below5e-16. These checks do not
constitute an independent audit of the whole model. The large computed
flow and its origin remain the immediate issue to examine before adding
physics or treating this diagnostic as a rejection of the broader approach.
