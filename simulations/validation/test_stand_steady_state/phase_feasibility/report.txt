Harmonic regenerator feasibility check, 2026-11-06

Decision: INCONCLUSIVE for the desired equilibrated coupling. Finite thermal
relaxation can matter under a low-flow heat-transfer estimate, but the tiny
operating-amplitude calculation invalidates that estimate and its Darcy-only
drag closure. This rejects those closures at that amplitude; it does not reject
the pressure/flow network approach. No production change is supported yet.

Scope and reproduction

Run .venv/Scripts/python -m
simulations.validation.test_stand_steady_state.phase_feasibility.check
from the repository root. check.py and results.json are the complete isolated
calculation. Latest numerical runtime: 0.75 s, about 3.7 s including interpreter
and imports. No fit, grid search, mean-profile equilibrium solve, or production
edit was performed. The four transport cases are one limit, one central
assumption, and two bracketing size assumptions, rather than a parameter search.

The prescribed mean profile is the archived placeholder equilibrium of the
CURRENT model at T0/T1/T2=295/58/3.4 K. It is not an equilibrium of this model.
Mean pressure is fixed at 1.565067 MPa, inlet peak sinusoidal pressure at 1 MPa,
frequency at 1 Hz, valve R=(1e10,3e10) Pa s/m^3. This pressure amplitude is
specified directly; no square-wave factor or compressor-supply normalization
is used. The sinusoidal inlet demand would be |m_in|/pi, but is not enforced.

Geometry and closures

Bulk dimensions, pore volumes, matrix masses and material capacities come from
library/heads/data/pt1_5.toml and the existing material library. Porosities 0.38
for spheres and 0.70 for screens are already estimates in the geometry source.
Particle diameters 0.2-0.5 mm (central 0.3 mm), screen wire diameters 30-60 um
(central 40 um), and screen tortuosity 2 are ASSUMPTIONS, not recovered PT1.5
measurements. Falter2024.pdf does not supply these dimensions in its text.
Related modern GM pulse-tube work uses 30 um wire and roughly 0.4-0.5 mm
spheres; that supports sensitivity scales, not identification of this head.

x increases warm to cold. Ag is pore area; Ab is bulk area; phi is porosity;
dp is sphere diameter; dw is wire diameter. The pressure-gradient convention
is dp_hat/dx=-(r+i*omega*l)*m_hat, with peak mass flux m_hat in kg/s.
r has units Pa s/(kg m); H has units W/(m K); l=1/Ag is used without a porous
tortuosity multiplier in this diagnostic. It is not a measured inertance.

For spheres, Ergun's low-flow permeability and wetted area give:
  K=phi^3*dp^2/[150*(1-phi)^2]
  r_D=mu/(rho*Ab*K)
  wetted area per length=6*(1-phi)*Ab/dp
  H=Nu*k/dp * wetted area per length
The network trial deliberately starts with Nu=2, a stagnant-sphere reference.
It is not a validated oscillatory packed-bed correlation.

For screens, the capillary-bundle estimate is:
  dh=phi*dw/(1-phi), K=phi*dh^2/(32*2)
  wetted area per length=4*(1-phi)*Ab/dw
  H=2*k/dh * wetted area per length
This is less secure than a measured screen permeability/transfer correlation.

Cg and Cm are gas and matrix heat capacities per length in J/(m K).
For spheres, both r_D and Nu=2 H scale as dp^-2: the 0.2-0.5 mm range is
0.36-2.25 times each central value. For screens, both scale as dw^-2 under
this chosen geometry model: the 30-60 um range is 0.44-1.78 times central.
These are closure sensitivities, not uncertainty confidence intervals.

HEPAK supplies rho, cp, compressibility, beta, mu and k. At 50 K it gives
rho=14.51 kg/m^3, mu=6.64e-6 Pa s and k=.0491 W/(m K). At 4 K it gives
mu=5.85e-6 Pa s and k=.02437 W/(m K). At 3.4 K its conductivity output is
zero, so this diagnostic explicitly holds k at its 4 K value below 4 K.
Its 3.4 K viscosity is finite, 5.79e-6 Pa s, but was not independently
validated. Cold-end precision below 4 K should not be inferred.

Amplitude-dependent scales change the picture

For Ergun's quadratic pressure gradient b*|m|*m,
 b=1.75*(1-phi)/(phi^3*dp*rho*Ab^2).
The fundamental of a sinusoidal |m|*m gives
 r_eff=r_D+[8/(3*pi)]*b*|m_hat|.
Thus a constant linear r from Darcy is a low-amplitude estimate only.

For an illustrative local mass amplitude .01 kg/s, the steady packed-sphere
Wakao reference Nu=2+1.1*Re^.6*Pr^(1/3), Re=|m_hat|*dp/(Ab*mu), gives:
 location       r_eff(Pa s/(kg m))    H(W/(m K))
 Pb,stage2 50K        6.63e7          7.50e4
 Er3Ni 32K            4.11e7          6.83e4
 HoCu2 15K            1.71e7          6.41e4
 GOS 4K               5.93e6          3.83e4
These are scale estimates only. The steady correlation is not established
here for cryogenic oscillatory flow. Nevertheless, physically plausible
convection raises H by tens of times, making omega*Cg/H and omega*Cm/H
correspondingly smaller, while inertial drag raises resistance by an order
of magnitude or more. It is unsafe to infer the operating model from either
Nu=2 thermal relaxation or Darcy resistance alone.

Tiny network result and validity gate

The trial solves the complex linear pressure/mass network with the proposed
gas/matrix equations, continuous junction pressure and conserved junction mass.
Each regenerator layer has 12 transfer cells; each ideal adiabatic pulse tube
has 24, a linear mean-temperature profile, no distributed resistance, and its
warm valve/reservoir termination. Stage energy includes gas enthalpy transport
and ideal pulse-tube acoustic removal. Wall conduction and breakthrough loss
are omitted from this directional transport-only check.

At the central low-Nu/Darcy assumptions, stage pressure amplitudes are .949
and .940 times inlet amplitude. Across the size bracket they are about .90-.97
times inlet amplitude. These are amplitude attenuation measures, not the
magnitude of complex pressure drop. Hydraulic attenuation in this low-drag
trial is already several percent, but missing inertial drag prevents using
it as an operating estimate.

The formal partial dQ1/dT2 is +28 W/K with matrix temperatures fixed, and
+57 W/K with a smooth endpoint interpolation through the whole stage-2
matrix. BOTH ARE UNRELAXED and physically inadmissible at this amplitude.
The corresponding transport stage powers are +1.14 kW and +4.33 kW, and
max |theta_g|/T in regenerator 2 is 22.3. Sphere Reynolds number reaches
about 9,000; omitted fundamental Forchheimer drag reaches 143 times Darcy.
The size bracket gives max |theta_g|/T about 16-37 and drag ratios 92-235.
The small-temperature-amplitude assumption fails decisively. Large power
imbalance also shows that the current-model mean profile is not suitable
as a claimed equilibrium of this model. The positive partials are recorded
to expose the failure, not as evidence of the required +1.4 W/K response.

Algebra check: elimination gives
 D=i*omega*Cg+H*i*omega*Cm/(H+i*omega*Cm)
 theta_g=(i*omega*beta*T*Ag*p_hat-cp*m_hat*T')/D.
As H tends to infinity, the pressure enthalpy coefficient becomes
1-beta*T/(1+Cg/Cm), agreeing with the current finite-capacity relation.
The gradient enthalpy flux is
 -0.5*cp^2*|m_hat|^2*Re(1/D)*T', positive towards the cold end when T'<0.
This verifies the thermal sign and limiting coefficient. It does not validate
the chosen transfer coefficients or the unrelaxed mean profile. Effective
coefficients must also respect peak sinusoidal versus f*mass-swing conventions;
the latter differs by pi in mass amplitude, hence pi^2 in a quadratic loss.

Next smallest meaningful decision test

Retain an isolated harmonic-balance network, with amplitude-dependent heat
transfer and Darcy-plus-Forchheimer drag, using a tightly bounded local solve.
First demand acceptable small temperature amplitudes, a balanced mean matrix
profile, conserved power/flow, and tolerable local stability. Only then use
the equilibrium Jacobian/Schur-complement gate to interpret stage coupling.
Keep drive and charge treatment explicit. No broad fit or production rewrite
should precede that test. If the small-amplitude gate continues to fail at the
operating drive, the first-order harmonic approximation itself needs revision.
