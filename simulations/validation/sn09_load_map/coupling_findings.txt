Observed decomposition

Let B = beta(T2, P_mean)*T2, where beta is helium's isobaric expansion coefficient
in 1/K, T2 is stage-2 temperature in K, and P_mean is mean pressure in Pa. Let H2
be the model's gross stage-2 enthalpy cooling power in W. Let D_c be its passive
regenerator boundary loss into stage 2, and W2 its tube-wall heat load, both in W.
The current model's available stage-2 cooling is

  Q2_available = B*H2 - D_c - W2.

For the 20 W stage-1 heater group, in increasing T2 order:

  T2/K       2.40568    3.31155    3.98343
  H2/W      19.99238   19.99238   19.99238
  B          0.03970    0.08785    0.14155
  B*H2/W     0.79372    1.75625    2.82994
  D_c/W      0.76496    0.62666    0.43210
  W2/W       0.28811    0.28941    0.29063
  Q2/W      -0.25935    0.84018    2.10721
  Heater/W   0.00000    0.50000    1.00000

Most of the upward curvature is in B*H2. Falling D_c reinforces it. Wall
conduction is almost constant. There is no explicit exponential in the cooling
formula; this diagnosis does not establish a particular analytic dependence on T2.

With no external matrix/clamp load, the total stage-2 column flow F is uniform
along the column. It is approximately 17.59 to 19.96 W in this group. The
stage-1 cooling balance receives +F, while stage 2 receives a loss -F:

  Q1_available = H1 - L1 + F - W1
  Q2_available = H2 - F - W2.

Here H1 is gross stage-1 enthalpy cooling, L1 is stage-1 regenerator loss, and W1
is net wall heat into stage 1, all in W. Thus the same modeled transport links the
stage-2 cooling curve and the stage-1 fit. This identity follows from the present
equations; it does not identify which constitutive approximation is inadequate.

Interpretation of the warm valve

The human recalls that helium nonideality was treated as important only at cold
temperatures. This is consistent with the real-fluid regenerator discussion below
about 30 K in Snodgrass et al. Constant H2 is not disproved by the valve being warm:
a lossless adiabatic pulse tube transmits constant cycle-averaged enthalpy power,
as Radebaugh's review explains in section 5.1. A new density conversion from warm
valve power to cold cooling should therefore not be introduced without deriving
the pulse-tube energy balance. Temperature-dependent losses or phase relations
remain possible, but were not quantified here.

Concrete candidate for further derivation

Snodgrass et al., Eq. (2), separates pressure-driven finite-matrix-capacity power
from temperature-gradient-driven transport. In their approximation the former is

  H_epsilon = epsilon/(1 + epsilon) * beta*T * E,

where epsilon is pore-gas heat capacity divided by solid-matrix heat capacity
for the same regenerator element, and E is local acoustic power, both powers in W.
Their Appendix C gives

  d(log(E))/dT = beta/(1 + epsilon).

The density-ratio acoustic scaling in the current ADR is the epsilon = 0 limit.
The current model has gradient-driven finite-capacity loss but no separate
H_epsilon term. Whether its empirical packing loss absorbs any of the omitted
effect requires a consistent derivation before changing the equations.

At the saved matrix-node states, epsilon is about 0.04 to 0.58 in the two
middle-load profiles. Evaluating the cold layer's pore-volume/matrix-mass ratio
and material properties at T2 gives a hypothetical cold-boundary H_epsilon scale
of 0.30, 0.63, and 1.02 W in the 20 W group, from low to high T2; the zero-W group
gives 0.24, 0.53, and 0.95 W. These are order-of-magnitude estimates using the
unchanged saved profiles and H2 as cold acoustic power, not a revised equilibrium
or a refit. Near-constant cold-end epsilon means this correction alone can largely
rescale B*H2; it does not guarantee a more linear map. Acoustic-power propagation
and the gradient loss must be treated consistently when testing this hypothesis.

Next proposed investigation: derive these two finite-capacity effects together
with the existing packing-loss approximation and pulse-tube boundary power, then
agree on any ADR change before implementing a replacement physical model. No
replacement has been agreed in chat; the current ADR remains authoritative.
