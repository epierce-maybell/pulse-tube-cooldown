SN09 bare-head cooldown: provisional first pass for task 2.5

Outcome

The experiment is implemented. The frozen candidate May fit does not reproduce
either cooldown from a uniform room-temperature initial state. Both integrations
reach a warm equilibrium with stage 2 near 12.6 K. The measured cold states are
not reached, so this is not a passing validation of task 2.5 or a basis for 2.6.

Run from the repository root:
    .venv/Scripts/python simulations/validation/sn09_bare_head/run.py

Inputs and assumptions

parameters.toml freezes the candidate joint May fit available on 2026-11-02.
may_fit_snapshot.json preserves that fit's diagnostics and source hashes. These
files are separate from the ongoing task 2.3 investigation. No fit is performed
by this runner. Both runs use the same four head parameters and 1.0 Hz frequency.

The network uses the existing PulseTubeCooler and engine, the two stage
inventories in TASKS 5.1, and each model layer's matrix Node. The clamp is the
HoCu2 layer, carrying only its matrix mass. All four tube walls use geometry.csv
and the fit's endpoint conventions. The stand links are tube walls only, matching
the fit; including chamber radiation through the MLI requires a matching refit.
The fixed warm boundary uses a copy of stage 1's composition to instantiate a
Node; that boundary's heat capacity does not affect its fixed temperature.

Each run starts uniformly at its first T3 reading (logged as TPR), which also
fixes the warm flange and fill temperature, following the fit's warm-temperature
convention. T3 is mounted on the pulse tube itself, as confirmed by the human
on 2026-11-02. Its initial reading serves as an assumed room-temperature proxy;
the records provide no direct warm-flange measurement.
The human identified its position as the main pulse tube reaching stage 2,
approximately 2 inches (0.0508 m) below stage 1. That approximate position is
recorded in geometry.csv; the sensor attachment details remain unspecified.
Time zero is the first recorded timestamp; no time shift was fitted. The May
record ends immediately before the appended restart at 14:19:17. Compressor
count is one for May and an assumed two for August; the lines, buffers, fill
pressure convention, and per-compressor values are unchanged. Drive estimates,
inventory sources, and comparison conventions are recorded in inputs.toml.

Each log residual is ln(model / measurement), with the model linearly
interpolated to the measurements' 2 s timestamps. Only finite T1 readings below
219 K and finite positive T2 readings enter the score. The initial T2 offset
(about 309 K measured versus about 294 K modeled) remains in that comparison.
T3 is plotted on its own as an unscored pulse-tube diagnostic. Its precise
mounting position has no corresponding thermal node in the present model.
The former T3/clamp overlay compared distinct physical locations and supplies
no evidence of clamp-temperature error. Results are reported per channel;
no acceptance threshold was supplied or inferred.

T1 cools appreciably too fast in both runs. T2 initially cools, then stalls near
12.6 K. At the final samples, the largest absolute temperature rate over free
nodes is about 5.6e-12 K/s in May and 3.8e-9 K/s in August. These are equilibria
under this model, rather than cooldowns that simply need more integration time.
The final pressure swing is 18.95 bar in May and the 19.30 bar relief cap in
August. Thus adding the second compressor has little effect at this equilibrium.

August load-map prediction

For each point, fix the warm flange and the measured stage temperatures and
solve the matrix-node balances, initialized from the cooldown sample closest in
log stage temperature. Predict available cooling as minus net flow into each
stage, including the walls; residual is net stage flow plus applied heater power.
Heater powers are comparison targets and never change the head parameters.

All six solves converge with maximum matrix imbalance below 1.7e-10 W.
Stage-1 residuals range from -0.65 to +12.56 W; stage-2 residuals range from
+20.22 to +30.39 W. Stage 2 has net heating at the measured stage temperatures
on these solved profiles. These equilibria were initialized from the transient
profiles; their dynamical stability and global branch reachability with fixed
plates have not been established. Other equilibrium branches can give different
load-map predictions. This is not a global search for every August equilibrium.

Interpretation and limits

The favorable May load-map residuals of the candidate fit do not establish
reachability of its cold branch during a complete cooldown. This first pass
exposes that gap under the stated initial state and stand assumptions. It does
not identify a unique incorrect physics term or settle the August compressor
count. Because the May transient itself misses, the August discrepancy cannot
isolate drive independence from the compressor-count assumption. The existing
2.3 investigation remains responsible for revisiting the fit and its branches.
